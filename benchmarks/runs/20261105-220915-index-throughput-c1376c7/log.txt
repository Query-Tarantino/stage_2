Factors: books per batch and indexer replicas, 5 repetitions each, interleaved: repetition 1 of every configuration, then repetition 2, so drift over the run spreads across every configuration
Trial: the corpus is ingested once; each trial recreates the indexers with its configuration and rebuilds the index into a new generation
Window: the whole one only, from the first index claim to the last batch published in the trial's generation; it leaves out the up to 30 s control takes to reindex the books and to switch generation

Series batch: 1, 10, 100 and 250 books per batch with 1 indexer replica
H1: books per second rise from 1 to 100 books per batch and flatten from 100 to 250, because each batch pays a fixed cost (its lease, the postings bulkWrites, publishing and marking) that more books share, while tokenizing and writing postings cost the same per book
Series replicas: 1, 2 and 3 indexer replicas with batches of 100
H2: books per second grow with replicas but less than linearly: three indexing threads do not run out of CPU, and every replica writes its postings to the same Mongo, which becomes the bottleneck
H3: batches of 250 fit in -Xmx512m without OutOfMemoryError, but the indexer's memory in stats.csv peaks higher than with batches of 100, because a batch keeps the term frequencies and postings of all its books until it writes them
